# WM001: Same humans

> Published on Nov 20, 2026

The first time, they talked about the first post. [HF001]

It ends with three short sentences. *None of this needed better people. The problem was never the people. It was the design.*

Balthazar had underlined "never" twice.

"Who designs the design?" he said, before his coffee had even arrived. "People. The same people. So the problem is the people again, just one step back."

"Of course people design it," said Caspar. "People designed sewers. In 1858 the Thames stank so badly that Parliament could hardly sit. Cholera kept coming back. Nobody made Londoners cleaner. An engineer built sewers, and the cholera stopped coming."

"Sewers don't have opinions," said Balthazar. "You can't lay a pipe for envy."

He tapped the title. "And *Designing New Humanity*. The last people who talked like that were the Soviets. The New Soviet Man. We know how that went."

"Read the next line," said Caspar. "*Same humans.* The Soviets tried to change the people to fit the plan. This is the other way round. You change the plan to fit the people."

"Every plan says that at the start."

Melchior put his cup down. "That's fair, actually. The test is what happens to the people who don't fit."

"They leave," said Caspar. "Or they start their own version next door. It says so."

"It says so," said Balthazar.

## The man on the phone

Balthazar told them about his week. On Tuesday a retired teacher had sent her savings to a man who said he was calling from her bank. He had known her address, her late husband's name and the last four digits of her card.

"People are very inventive," Balthazar said, "when it comes to taking things from other people."

"What was he after?" said Melchior.

"Money. Obviously."

"So in a world without money—"

"Don't."

Melchior didn't. He just waited.

"Fine," said Balthazar. "He wanted money. But money was only the door. Behind it is what money gets you. Being somebody. Being looked at. Having people do what you say. Take the money away and he'll go for those directly. MJ admits it, in a list near the end. A list near the end isn't a solution."

## Christmas presents

"I sold drills for twenty-six years," said Melchior. "When the tool library opened, everyone said nobody would bring them back."

"And?"

"Most people did. The library lost a few. I lost a few to shoplifters too, and I'd bet some of them were the same people. But hundreds of people stopped buying a drill they'd use for thirteen minutes in its whole life."

"Nobody knows where that number comes from," said Balthazar.

"No," Melchior admitted. "But I know how many drills I sold as Christmas presents."

Caspar laughed. Balthazar nearly did.

"Nobody preached to them about sharing," Melchior went on. "The library was just easier than my shop. That's the design. And the shoplifters still shoplifted. That's the people."

## The last few coins

"In 2020," said Caspar, "Luxembourg made every bus, tram and train in the country free. A whole country. Nobody had to become a better person to get on a bus."

"Taxes were already paying about ninety percent of it," said Balthazar. "They just stopped collecting the last few coins."

"Yes!" Caspar looked delighted. "That's exactly how it happens. Each step is small, because of all the steps before it." [HF006]

"So what are we actually arguing about?" said Melchior.

Balthazar thought about it. "How much. MJ says never. I'd say it's half the people and half the setup."

"Ninety-ten," said Caspar.

"Nobody knows," said Melchior. "Nobody has ever run the experiment for long enough."

Caspar leaned in. "So give it three generations. Children who have never had to fight for food, or a roof, or a job—"

"Will find something else to fight over," said Balthazar.

"Probably," said Caspar. "Benches. Who gets the lake cabin in July. Fine. Nobody ever lost their home over a bench."

Balthazar opened his mouth, and closed it again.

The other two never did agree. Melchior agreed with both of them, and finished his coffee.

## Where they left it

**Balthazar:** "Same humans" isn't the comfort MJ thinks it is. It's the problem. Still no.

**Melchior:** You can't design good people. You can stop paying people to be bad. That will do, for him.

**Caspar:** He'd change "never" to "almost never". He wouldn't change anything else.

* * *

I've thought about changing "never" myself. I've left it. A first post is allowed to be a little too sure of itself.

Melchior paid. Caspar had forgotten his wallet again. Balthazar said that was exactly his point.

Nobody asked him which one.

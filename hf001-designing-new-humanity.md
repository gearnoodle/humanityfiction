# HF001: Designing New Humanity

> Published on Oct 25, 2026

![Layered Paper-Cut Community Garden Utopia.jpg](https://humanityfiction.mataroa.blog/images/7dc20527.jpeg)

Same humans. Same planet. Same bad singing at birthdays. Only one thing has changed: the way people live together was finally designed on purpose.

If you arrived there from 2026, this is what you'd notice, roughly in this order.

**In the first minute:** the quiet. Hardly any cars. No adverts. Nobody running for anything.

**In the first hour:** nothing has a price. You look for somewhere to pay for your coffee, and there isn't one. The person who made it doesn't understand the question.

**On the first day:** nobody asks what you do. They ask what you're into.

**In the first week:** things are clearly getting done, but you can't find who's in charge. Nobody is. A shared page called the Earth-monitor shows what's broken and what's needed. People pick something when they feel like it. Not everyone does. Enough do.

**In the first month:** you get tired of resting and fix a wobbly bench. Nobody asked you to, which is exactly why you felt like it.

**In the first year:** you stop patting your pockets for keys and a wallet.

## What you'd never notice

The rules, because there are only three: don't harm, don't deceive, and don't block others.

Everything else is optional. Helping, finishing what you start, even joining in at all. The whole thing is a hobby.

## What hasn't changed

People. They're still grumpy, shy, lonely and wrong about things, the ordinary amount. Drains still block. Nobody gets more hours in a day, and nobody can be made to love anybody.

It isn't a perfect world. It's a floor nobody can fall through, with the ceiling left open.

## What's left of 2026

Not much. Money and scarcity have mostly been phased out. Nobody banned them. They slowly stopped being needed, the way phone boxes did.

A few leftovers are kept around on purpose: a museum, an old wallet, a rental flat with the landlord's angry notes in a frame, and one man who still tries to sell courgettes. They're there so people remember how odd it used to be.

## The one idea

None of this needed better people.

The old world kept asking people to be kinder, greener and harder-working, inside a setup that made all of that difficult. This one stopped asking. It made being decent the easy option.

The problem was never the people. It was the design.

* * *

This blog is a thought exercise about that design, told in small stories from one small town.

Don't take it too seriously.

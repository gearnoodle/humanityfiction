# HF031: The short version

> Published on Nov 11, 2026

Leena has lived above the bike repair shop for a month now. Yesterday she asked what I'd actually worked out in two years here.

I said I'd written some of it down.

"All of it?"

"The parts that fit on a bench."

So I read her these.

## The one idea

> The problem was never the people. It was the design.
> — [HF001]

> It was like building a restaurant around the needs of the cash register.
> — [HF017]

> If a job is so bad that nobody would ever choose it, that's not a people problem. It's a design problem.
> — [HF010]

> You can fight a revolution. It's much harder to fight a shrug.
> — [HF018]

## Nothing is expected

> Disappointing someone isn't the same as hurting them. If I expected you to finish and you didn't, that's my expectation, not your crime.
> — [HF003]

> Stop whenever you like. Vanish if you must. Just don't leave a trap, a lie, or a locked door.
> — [HF003]

> Stopping is not the opposite of progress. Sometimes it's how progress happens.
> — [HF021]

> Small contributions aren't the warm-up. They're the whole thing.
> — [HF004]

## Nobody in charge

> The best defence against exploitation isn't more rules. It's the freedom to walk away.
> — [HF005]

> In the old world, the person in charge of a big project had power. Here, they have a to-do list.
> — [HF012]

> Take the threat away, and hierarchy turns out to be quite pleasant for some people.
> — [HF013]

> It does not say, Go. It only makes the world difficult to dismiss.
> — [HF002]

> In the old world, screens were designed to keep you looking. This one is designed to let you go.
> — [HF007]

> Maybe that's the real test of any system: how easy is it to leave?
> — [HF015]

## Enough

> "Nobody switched," Aino said. "One day you just noticed you hadn't paid for anything in a while."
> — [HF006]

> The word "deserve" confuses them. "Why would you have to deserve food?"
> — [HF009]

> If everyone did about 3 hours a month, everything that truly needs doing would get done.
> — [HF011]

> Places aren't better or worse anymore. They're just different.
> — [HF016]

## Each other

> Seen this way, rudeness is just a gift not given. You can wish for a gift, but you can't demand one.
> — [HF008]

> Faults, you punish. Causes, you fix.
> — [HF019]

> "Knowing why someone broke your window doesn't mean you have to let them into your house."
> — [HF020]

> Every other kind of abundance in this world was built. Love can't be. It can only be offered, again and again, by ordinary people who don't have to.
> — [HF014]

## What the design can't do

> It isn't a perfect world. It's a floor nobody can fall through, with the ceiling left open.
> — [HF001]

> The goal is not to make humans better at enduring badly designed experiences.
> — [HF023]

> "Needs are not the same as longings," Aino replied. "And longings are not always problems to be solved."
> — [HF025]

> "Is it okay if I don't know what I'm for yet?"
>
> "Yes," she said. "It's more than okay. It's the usual condition."
> — [HF025]

> Perhaps people should also have the freedom to have a rather mediocre Tuesday.
> — [HF024]

## Leave it tilted

> "Because if I fix everything that can be fixed," he said, "I will eventually run out of world. And I am not finished looking at this one yet."
> — [HF026]

> Some resistances, he was beginning to understand, are not problems in the system.
>
> They are how the system knows it is still made of people.
> — [HF027]

> Perhaps we can play with the problem without playing with the consequences.
> — [HF022]

> "Do you think it knows where it's going?"
>
> "I doubt it," Kevin replied.
>
> "Good," she said. "It's more interesting that way."
> — [HF028]

## And for anyone reading from 2026

> Getting better at something is play. Being told to get better *as a person* is a debt, and nobody ever tells you the amount.
> — [HF030]

> The leaves were already free. Did you look?
> — [HF029]

> It might be the most honest shared story humans ever had. It admits it's a story.
> — [HF018]

> Don't take it too seriously.
> — [HF001]

* * *

Leena asked which one I'd keep, if I could only keep one.

I thought about it.

"Probably the one about the bench."

"Which one about the bench?"

"Any of them."

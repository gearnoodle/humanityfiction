# HF032: A good bench

> Published on Nov 11, 2026

I've sat on the pink bench most days for two years. Yesterday it occurred to me that I keep asking what makes a good life, and the bench has been answering the whole time. I just wasn't looking down.

## What a good bench is

**It doesn't go anywhere.** Everything else in town is on its way somewhere. The bench is useful because it stays put.

**It was never finished.** M. fixed one leg and moved away. I sanded the slats. Somebody ran out of green and used pink. It was a bench the whole time.

**Nobody made it alone.** There are at least five people in it, and a robot who decided to leave it be.

**It leans a little.** Two millimetres to the left. It holds people anyway.

**Half its wobble is the ground.** A lot of what looks wrong with a bench is what it's standing on.

**It faces something.** A library door, some tomatoes, the occasional cat. It doesn't need a sunset. It needs somewhere to look.

**It has room for someone else.** That's the difference between a bench and a chair.

**It sits out in the weather.** It needs a bit of care now and then. Not constantly. Fourteen coats of varnish, and it would be mostly varnish.

**Nobody asks what it has achieved.** People ask whether it holds. It holds.

## And a person

I tried the list on myself.

Not going anywhere in particular. Never finished. Made by a lot of other people. Leaning slightly. Wobbling mostly because of the ground. Facing something. Room for one more. Needing a little care, but not much.

It fits better than any list I was given in the old world. Those were all about being a faster vehicle.

The old world liked to say that life is a journey. From here it looks more like a place to sit: somewhere a friend can stop for a while, or a child with a jar of beetles, or a grumpy man working up to his first word.

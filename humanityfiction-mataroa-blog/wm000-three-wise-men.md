# WM000: Three wise men

> Published on Nov 20, 2026

I once admitted that none of my friends are really into this. When it comes up, they mostly see humanity's flaws. [HF033]

That's still true. But a blog with only one voice in it soon turns into a sermon. So I've invited three readers to argue with it.

From now on, every so often, a post will be theirs. These are the WM posts, for Wise Men.

## Are they real?

No. They're made up, but out of real parts. Every argument they make is one I've heard from someone, or had with myself at six in the morning. The facts they use are real, and I check them. Their names are borrowed.

Like everything in the town, this says how it was made. [HF041]

I named them after the three kings in the Christmas story, because of what each one brings. They meet on Thursday mornings in a café near the station. In this world, you still have to pay for the coffee.

## Balthazar brings myrrh

Myrrh was the strange gift. It's a bitter resin, and people used it to prepare the dead. An odd thing to bring a baby.

Balthazar is 34, the youngest. He works on a bank's fraud team. All day he talks to people who have just sent their savings to a stranger on the phone. He grew up with one crisis after another: the crash, the rent, the climate, then whatever came next. He has never seen the future get better, and he doesn't see why it would start now.

He thinks this blog describes a world that can't happen, because people are what they are. He reads every post anyway. He says it's to find out where it breaks.

Every Thursday he comes to bury the future. So far, it keeps sitting up.

## Melchior brings gold

Melchior is 58. For twenty-six years he ran a hardware shop. Then a tool library opened two streets away and started lending drills for free. It wasn't the only reason his shop closed, but it didn't help. He hated that library. Now he volunteers there two mornings a week.

So he has lost something to the kind of world this blog describes, and he has also watched it work.

He brings gold, for the golden mean. Aristotle, an ancient Greek philosopher, thought a good thing usually sits between two bad ones: courage, for example, sits between running away and running in without looking. Melchior listens to both of the others and keeps the best of each. When they won't agree with each other, he agrees with both of them, privately, and goes home content.

He doesn't think the answer is always in the middle. He's just annoyed by how often it turns out to be.

## Caspar brings frankincense

Frankincense is burnt, and the smoke goes up. Caspar is always going up.

He's 76, the oldest. He spent forty years as a water engineer, laying pipes and digging wells in places that didn't have them. In 1950, the year he was born, almost one child in four in the world died before turning five. Now it's fewer than one in twenty-five. He saw smallpox wiped out. He saw clean water reach villages, and he dug some of it himself.

So when Balthazar says people never change, Caspar thinks he has watched them do it.

He likes this blog, sometimes more than I do. He thinks most of it could happen, and some of it soon. He's often too quick. But he knows how long it takes to lay a pipe, and he never forgets that somebody has to dig the trench. His head is in the clouds. His feet aren't.

He always forgets his wallet. He says he's practising. [HF001]

## The rules

- Each WM post looks at one HF post.
- Nobody has to agree, and nobody wins.
- Balthazar always gets a fair hearing. If the town can't survive his questions, better to find out now.
- At the end, I write down where each of them left it.

I'm not sure any of them is wise. But there are three of them, and they keep bringing gifts nobody asked for.

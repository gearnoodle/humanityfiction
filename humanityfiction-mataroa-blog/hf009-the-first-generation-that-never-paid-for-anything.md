# HF009: The first generation that never paid for anything

> Published on Oct 25, 2026

Eevi is nine. Yesterday she found my old wallet in a drawer and asked what it was for.

I tried to explain. She listened politely, the way you listen to someone describing a strange dream.

Her generation has never paid for anything. Talking to them about the old world is like being interviewed by very friendly aliens.

## Things they find strange about us

- **Money.** "So you gave someone numbers, and then they let you eat?"
- **Bike locks.** "But what if someone else needed it?"
- **Things that broke on purpose.** "On *purpose*?" She made me repeat this one three times.
- **Jobs.** "You went somewhere you didn't like, every day, for forty years? Were you being punished?"
- **Weekends.** "Why only two days?"
- **Storage units.** "You paid for a room to keep things you didn't use?"
- **Advertising.** "Strangers paid to make you want stuff? And you let them?"
- **Tipping.** "You paid extra so people would be nice to you?"
- **Retirement.** "You had to get old before you were allowed to do what you liked?"
- **The doctor.** This one she didn't laugh at. "People stayed sick because of a bill?" She was quiet for a while after that.

## How they think

They don't ask "how much does it cost?" They ask "how long would it take?" and "who needs it?"

They don't ask "whose is this?" They ask "is anyone using it?"

The word "deserve" confuses them. "Why would you have to deserve food?"

They're generous without noticing. To them, giving something away isn't especially kind. It's just what you do with things you're not using.

## What they're bad at

To be fair, they have blind spots too.

They're terrible at waiting in line, because they've hardly ever had to. They don't understand saving things "for later". And when something really is scarce, like the one sunny spot on the pink bench, they're baffled. Last week two of them argued over it for twenty minutes, then started building a second bench.

They also don't quite believe us. To them, the old world sounds like a story adults tell to make children grateful.

* * *

In the end, Eevi asked if she could keep my wallet.

She uses it for storing interesting leaves.

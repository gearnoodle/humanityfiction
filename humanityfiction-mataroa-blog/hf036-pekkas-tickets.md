# HF036: Pekka's tickets

> Published on Nov 12, 2026

Pekka spoke to me on Tuesday.

This is news. In two years he hadn't said a word to me, and in twenty he hasn't said many to anybody. Marja is somewhere past a hundred and forty hours with him and counts a grunt as progress.

"I've read your notes," he said. "All of them."

Then he handed me a pad. In the old world Pekka was a parking enforcement officer, and he still has several hundred blank tickets. He had filled in ten.

I've copied them here with my answers. I tried to be fair. He checked.

## Ticket 1

> **Offence:** "Three hours a month." Who counted?

Nobody, exactly. It's an estimate in the corner of the Earth-monitor, and it moves. Last February it said eleven. People don't quote February.

**Upheld.**

## Ticket 2

> **Offence:** "Sensors count, they never identify." Three people sat on the bench today. One of them sits there at six every morning. It doesn't need my name.

He's right. In a small town, enough counting is watching. The data stays coarse, your page stays on your own device, and you can switch it all off. But the sentence I wrote was tidier than the truth.

**Upheld.**

## Ticket 3

> **Offence:** Claims people will work without being paid or made to. They won't.

Pekka has swept the library steps every morning for twenty years. Nobody asked him to.

"That's different," he said. "They were dirty."

**Dismissed.**

## Ticket 4

> **Offence:** Everyone in these notes is pleasant.

I write about the people I like. I've left out the man who explains things, the gossip on Mill Street, and a feud in the choir that is older than Eevi.

"You left me out," Pekka said.

"I put you in."

"Sitting down. Once."

**Upheld.**

## Ticket 5

> **Offence:** Nothing in these notes ever breaks. A wet meadow. A notebook.

Three winters ago the heating on the north side was off for eleven days. Sixty people were signed up to fix it. Each of them assumed that one of the other fifty-nine had gone.

I wrote that forty people standing behind you makes stopping safe. It also makes forty people think someone else has it.

**Upheld.**

## Ticket 6

> **Offence:** "Nobody has to do anything." Then why is it always Aino?

It is quite often Aino. And Raili, and Kevin, and about nine others. The Earth-monitor shows this. Showing it hasn't changed it.

**Partly upheld.**

## Ticket 7

> **Offence:** Reputation is money that has learned manners.

It can't be saved, spent, or left to your children, and it only works in one subject. That's a real difference. But people do go quiet when Raili talks about drains, including people who are right.

**Partly upheld.**

## Ticket 8

> **Offence:** "You can always leave." To where? Everywhere is like this.

Towns differ more than he thinks. But what keeps people anywhere is other people, and you can't take those with you. Leaving is cheap for Leena. It isn't cheap for someone with one friend.

**Partly upheld.**

## Ticket 9

> **Offence:** Does not say who stops a man who won't stop.

Whoever is nearest. With their hands, if it comes to that. It has come to that here, twice that I know of. Nobody is proud of it, and nobody has written it down properly, including me.

**No good answer yet.**

## Ticket 10

> **Offence:** "Causes, not faults." I wrote more tickets than anyone in the district for thirty years. You say it was the design. Then it wasn't me either.

I read this one several times.

We tell people their worst years weren't their fault. I hadn't thought about what that takes away. Pekka was good at something, everyone hated him for it, and now we've told him it was never really his.

**No answer.**

## The count

Upheld: four. Partly upheld: three. Dismissed: one. No answer: two.

I asked why he stays, if he thinks all that.

"Somebody has to check," he said.

* * *

I asked what I owed him for the tickets.

"Nothing. Nothing costs anything here." He put the pad back in his coat. "That's eleven."

Marja says it counts as four hours.

# HF013: Hierarchy as a hobby

> Published on Nov 1, 2026

Heikki worked thirty years in a warehouse. Someone always told him what to do. He hated it, mostly.

Then the new world arrived, and nobody told him anything at all. He found that even harder. Every morning the Earth-monitor offered him three small tasks and a whole free day.

"Too many choices," he told me on the pink bench. "I just want someone to say: Heikki, carry this. Heikki, go there."

So on Monday, he signed up for one season with the Flood Army.

## A different kind of army

The Flood Army has ranks, drills and orders. Its uniforms are bright orange and covered in patches from earlier seasons. Its captain this autumn is Sanna, who on any other day is the quiet librarian. In the library, she whispers. On the riverbank, she shouts.

Heikki loves it. He gets told where to stand, what to carry, and when to eat. "It's like a holiday for my head," he said.

But it isn't an old-world army:

- **Anyone can leave, any day.** Nobody is punished. Signing up for a season is just a nice way to plan.
- **Rank stays inside.** A captain on Tuesday is just someone on a bench on Wednesday. Orders only count for the army's own task.
- **Authority is borrowed.** A leader only leads while people choose to follow. If they leave, the rank means nothing.
- **The three rules still apply.** No order can make anyone harm, deceive or block. No army has any power over people who aren't in it.
- **No weapons.** "Army" here just means a well-organised crowd of helpers.

## Why anyone would want this

In the old world, most hierarchy came with a threat attached. Obey, or lose your job, your pay, your home. No wonder people hated it.

Take the threat away, and hierarchy turns out to be quite pleasant for some people. Nobody thinks an orchestra is oppressed by its conductor. The conductor is what makes the music possible.

Some people join for the relief of not choosing, like Heikki. Some, like Sanna, love turning chaos into order. Some come for the drills, which they say feel like dancing. Most stay for the soup and singing afterwards.

## Anyone can start one

If a cause gets enough people interested, an army forms. If nobody joins, it fades away.

- **The Tree Army** plants forests. The old world had one too: in the 1930s, the US Civilian Conservation Corps planted so many trees that people called it "Roosevelt's Tree Army".
- **The Flood Army, the Earthquake Army and the Firebreak Army** get places ready before disasters arrive.
- **The Loneliness Army** visits people who'd like company. The courgette man was recruit number one.
- **The Quiet Army** protects silent places.
- **The Drain Army** has Kevin the robot as honorary sergeant.

## When it goes wrong

Structure plus loyalty can turn into a cult, or a gang. It has happened.

Last year, one small army started shaming people who wanted to leave. It fell apart within a week. Everyone could see what was happening on the Earth-monitor, and nobody could be stopped from walking away. So they did.

The defences are the same as everywhere else in this world: easy exit, everything visible, no power over outsiders, and rank that dissolves when the job is done.

* * *

The season ended last Friday. Sanna gave Heikki a patch for his uniform and went back to whispering in the library.

On Saturday morning, Heikki looked at his three tasks and picked one himself: watering the tomatoes outside the library. He looked very proud of himself.

He's signed up for spring anyway.
